# Known open defects

Defects present in the current tree and not yet fixed, most severe first. Each entry names the cause, the affected code, what it costs today, and what would raise its severity.

## The alpha convention is a label callers set, so a wrong, dropped or ignored label colours an image wrongly
An image's pixels are held in one of two conventions, `AlphaMode.STRAIGHT` (each colour at full strength, what a file holds) or `AlphaMode.PREMULTIPLIED` (each colour already scaled by its alpha, what drawing source-over onto a transparent surface leaves). The fill of a Minecraft tooltip is the worked example: straight it is `F0100010`, (16, 0, 16) at alpha 240; premultiplied it is `F00F000F`, (15, 0, 15) at 240. The convention lives on `ImageData` as a label - `getAlphaMode()`, defaulting to `STRAIGHT`, set through `StaticImageData.of(PixelBuffer, AlphaMode)` and `AnimatedImageData.Builder#withAlphaMode` - while `PixelBuffer`, which holds the pixels, carries none, so every operation on a buffer takes its caller's word. The readers, the writers (`toPixelBuffer(AlphaMode.STRAIGHT)`), `toPixelBuffer(AlphaMode)`, `toAlphaMode` and `toBufferedImage()` convert correctly from the label; the failures are all the label being wrong, missing or ignored. asset-renderer, which draws premultiplied canvases from straight textures, has hit all three: a hand-written `ImageData` inheriting the `STRAIGHT` default dropped a render's mark and wrote its see-through pixels a level dark; a background composited in the image's label over unlabelled premultiplied renders darkened them twice (`80800000` over `FF404040` gave `FF602020` for `FFA02020`); and its frame compositor still lays a placed image's held pixels down with `BlendMode.PREMULTIPLIED` whatever that image's label, so a straight see-through child encodes too bright (`505000FF` writes as `50FF00FF`). Each fix there passes an `AlphaMode` by hand at the site that builds the image.

Why the label cannot simply be deleted, settled before any design is written:
- **Storage layout does not touch it.** A packed `int` is already four bytes, one a channel; separate planes, a `byte[]` or floats for alpha alone hold the same values. The red byte still holds 16 or 15, and which one is the convention.
- **One convention everywhere costs accuracy at 8 bits.** Premultiplying a decoded texel loses colour at low alpha - (200, 100, 50) at alpha 10 holds (8, 4, 2) and reads back (204, 102, 51) - and a renderer matching a GPU blends a straight texel once (`src * a + dst * (1 - a)`, rounded once), not a texel rounded on load and again on the blend. A GPU's own framebuffer, cleared transparent and drawn with source-alpha blending, holds premultiplied colour. So a byte-exact renderer reads straight sources and writes premultiplied canvases, and both kinds of buffer exist.
- **More bits per channel** (16-bit, float) shrink the rounding but leave the choice, cost two to four times the memory, and an 8-bit target still rounds where it rounds.
- **Two types** - a straight image and a premultiplied canvas - remove the label at compile time, but split `PixelBuffer` and `ImageData` and every signature that names them.

The direction chosen: one class, with the convention a property of the `PixelBuffer`, set by how the buffer is made and never passed by a caller.
- **Readers** answer `STRAIGHT` buffers, since every format stores straight colour (GIF's on/off transparency holds the same either way).
- **A canvas factory**, `PixelBuffer.canvas(width, height)`, answers a transparent `PREMULTIPLIED` buffer. Wrapping a hand-built `int[]` is the one case the library cannot infer, so `of` splits into two named factories rather than taking a parameter.
- **`wrap(BufferedImage)`** reads the convention off the image: `TYPE_INT_ARGB` and `TYPE_4BYTE_ABGR` are straight, `TYPE_INT_ARGB_PRE` and `TYPE_4BYTE_ABGR_PRE` premultiplied, a custom layout says so through `ColorModel#isAlphaPremultiplied()`. A `_PRE` image could then be shared zero-copy as `TYPE_INT_ARGB` is; today it is copied and un-premultiplied to straight with the library's own rounding.
- **Every operation that reads two buffers honours both**: `blit` and `blitScaled` lay a source down in the destination's convention, converting a straight source on the way, so the compositor defect above cannot be written. The bicubic resample already declares `TYPE_INT_ARGB_PRE` for a premultiplied source. `toBufferedImage()` answers `TYPE_INT_ARGB_PRE` sharing the array for a premultiplied buffer, and the writers convert at the edge as they do now.
- **`ImageData#getAlphaMode()`** answers what its frames' buffers hold, every frame agreeing; the `AlphaMode` parameters of `StaticImageData.of` and `withAlphaMode` go, and `toAlphaMode` / `toPixelBuffer(AlphaMode)` stay as conversions.
- **Equality** counts the convention: the same ints under two conventions are two pictures.
- **Raw writes stay the caller's**: `setPixel`, `data()` and a loop over them must write in the buffer's convention, and `PixelGraphics`, which draws through Java2D, presents a premultiplied buffer as a `TYPE_INT_ARGB_PRE` image. This is the one place the convention is still honoured by hand.

In asset-renderer the change deletes the `AlphaMode` argument of `Timeline#wrap`, the labels `Timeline#bake`, `Timeline.still` and `FrameCompositor.merge` set, and the label arguments to `StaticImageData.of`; a renderer draws on a canvas and the convention travels with its pixels. It is a public API change to `PixelBuffer`, `ImageData`, `StaticImageData`, the `AnimatedImageData` builder and `PixelGraphics`, published through JitPack, then a pass through each consumer. Not started. HIGH once a consumer composites a straight see-through image through a path that ignores its label.

- Affected: `src/main/java/dev/simplified/image/PixelBuffer.java`, `src/main/java/dev/simplified/image/ImageData.java` (`getAlphaMode`, `toPixelBuffer(AlphaMode)`, `toAlphaMode`, `toBufferedImage`), `src/main/java/dev/simplified/image/StaticImageData.java` (`of`), `src/main/java/dev/simplified/image/AnimatedImageData.java` (`Builder#withAlphaMode`), `src/main/java/dev/simplified/image/pixel/AlphaMode.java`, `src/main/java/dev/simplified/image/pixel/PixelGraphics.java`
- Severity: **MEDIUM**
- Type: **RISK**
- Status: **OPEN**

## PixelGraphics' fills and shapes overwrite the pixel while it reports `SrcOver`
`PixelGraphics` extends `Graphics2D`, starts with `AlphaComposite.SrcOver` and hands back from `getComposite` whatever `setComposite` stored, but no colour draw reads it. `fillDeviceRect` sends every rectangle and every row a fill, outline or shape lays at once to `PixelBuffer#fillRect(int, int, int, int, int)`, an overwrite, and `setPixelSafe` sends each pixel a line, oval, arc (`fillArc` included) or rounded corner plots alone to `setPixel`; only the `drawImage` forms composite, with `BlendMode.NORMAL`. A translucent colour therefore replaces what lies beneath it rather than laying over it: a `0x80FF0000` fill over a premultiplied `F00F000F` stores `80FF0000`, where `BlendMode.NORMAL`, and Java2D's `SrcOver` on a `TYPE_INT_ARGB_PRE` image, store `F8870007`. Several draws also plot a pixel more than once in one call, which an overwrite hides and a composite would not, since a pixel laid twice takes the colour twice: `drawRect`'s four edges share their corners (and lie one on another when it has no width or height), `drawOval` plots the points on each axis twice, `drawRoundRect`'s corner arcs repeat the end pixels of its straight edges (with both arc sizes below 2 its edges meet at their corners as `drawRect`'s do), `fillRoundRect`'s body shares its top and bottom rows with the corner bands, `drawPolyline` and `drawPolygon` repeat each shared vertex, `fillPolygon` can repeat a node two spans share, `drawArc`'s sampled points can repeat, and `draw(Shape)` repeats the point where two of its segments meet, a curve being drawn as many short lines. No workspace caller draws a translucent fill or shape through a `PixelGraphics`: asset-renderer's text decorations go through minecraft-text's own `fillEffect`, and minecraft-text calls none of the fills it inherits. HIGH once a caller draws a translucent fill or shape through one.

- Affected: `src/main/java/dev/simplified/image/pixel/PixelGraphics.java:124-127` (`setPixelSafe`), `:150-162` (`fillDeviceRect`), `:291-298` (`fillRect`, `clearRect`), `:301-692` (outlines and shapes), `:699-727` (`drawQuadCurve`, `drawCubicCurve`), `:966-973` (`setComposite`, `getComposite`)
- Severity: **MEDIUM**
- Type: **BUG**
- Status: **OPEN**

## No ICC / Exif / XMP metadata model; WebP ICC profiles are dropped
`WebPChunk.Type` names `ICCP`, `EXIF` and `XMP `, but no reader or writer carries them and `ImageData` has no metadata field, so colour profiles and Exif are lost on every WebP round trip. MEDIUM once a wide-gamut or HDR format depends on its colour metadata.

- Affected: `src/main/java/dev/simplified/image/codec/webp/WebPChunk.java:39-54`, `src/main/java/dev/simplified/image/codec/webp/WebPImageReader.java`, `src/main/java/dev/simplified/image/codec/webp/WebPImageWriter.java`, `src/main/java/dev/simplified/image/ImageData.java`
- Severity: **LOW**
- Type: **GAP**
- Status: **OPEN**
